How to budget without linking your bank account

Most budgeting apps start by asking for your online-banking login. You don't have to hand it over. Manual entry plus an occasional statement import gives you a complete, accurate budget, and many people find they understand their spending better this way.

Updated 2026-10-13 · 6 min read

Why people skip the bank link

  • Credentials and access. Linking usually goes through a third-party aggregator that holds ongoing access to your accounts. Some banks warn that sharing your login can affect your protection if something goes wrong. Check your bank's terms.
  • Connections break. Bank links drop, need re-authentication, double-import transactions, or stop working for smaller banks and credit unions.
  • Automatic feeds make you passive. When every coffee is imported for you, it's easy to never actually look. Typing a purchase, even in five seconds, makes you notice it.
  • Cash doesn't show up anyway. If part of your income or spending is cash, a bank feed is incomplete no matter what.

Two ways to get transactions in

1. Log as you spend (seconds per purchase)

Enter the amount, pick a category, done. A good app suggests the category from what you've entered before, so most entries come down to an amount and a tap. Do it right after paying, while you're still at the till, or in one pass at the end of the day.

2. Import your bank statement (once a week or month)

Every bank lets you download your transactions from its website, with no login shared with anyone:

  1. Sign in to your bank's website yourself and open the account.
  2. Find Download, Export or Download transactions (sometimes under the account's activity or statements page).
  3. Pick a date range and a format. QFX (Quicken) or OFX is the most reliable if offered; otherwise choose CSV.
  4. Import the file into your budgeting app and review the rows before saving.

Most people combine the two: log day-to-day spending as it happens, then import the statement monthly to catch anything missed, like automatic payments, fees or interest.

Setting up the budget

  1. Start from real numbers. Import the last two or three months of statements, then look at what you actually spend per category. Budget from that, not from what you wish you spent.
  2. Set a monthly limit per category. Groceries, rent, transport, eating out, subscriptions. Keep it to the categories you'd actually change.
  3. Check in weekly. Five minutes: anything over budget? Anything recurring you forgot about?
  4. Reconcile against your bank balance. Once a month, your app's account balance should match your bank's. If it doesn't, the statement import shows you what's missing.
